2. Data We Process

PinChat is designed to process as little data as possible, and to keep none of it on disk. In the normal operation of the Service, the following data is processed:

  • IP address and User-Agent string. Technical metadata that any HTTPS request exposes. (log retention policy not configured) Your IP address in the clear is held in server memory only for the duration of each request or active connection. For rate limiting, the server also keeps a keyed hash (HMAC-SHA256) of your IP address in memory for the length of the relevant rate-limit window, at most 15 minutes after your last request; the key is generated at random every time the server starts, so the hashes cannot be correlated across restarts. See Section 4 for what the hosting provider may retain independently.
  • Beta access password. During the beta phase, access is gated by an access password shared by the beta participants. The password is not linked to any person by the Service and is stored only as an Argon2id hash. The Controller hands the password to beta participants directly, through the channel on which they were already in contact (for example email or an instant-messaging application); the Controller therefore knows who received it, but the Service itself does not, and nothing you do on the Service is associated with that channel. That correspondence is treated as described under "Correspondence" below.
  • Session cookie (pinchat_session). A random, server-side session identifier set after the beta password is accepted. HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Strict. Strictly necessary to keep you authenticated; not shared with anyone.
  • CSRF cookie (csrf_token). A short-lived (15 minutes), HMAC-signed token that protects the access form against cross-site request forgery. Strictly necessary; cleared immediately after access is granted.
  • Proof-of-work cookie (pc_pow). A random identifier, valid for 5 minutes, HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Strict, that ties a proof-of-work challenge to your browser so that people sharing the same public IP address do not overwrite each other's challenges. Strictly necessary; it carries no information about you and is not used for tracking.
  • Room metadata. Room identifier (UUID), room type, time-to-live, creation and last-activity timestamps, maximum and current number of participants. Held exclusively in server memory for the life of the room, never written to disk, deleted automatically when the room expires.
  • Proof-of-work challenge cache. A record keyed by the hash of your IP address combined with the pc_pow cookie value, kept in memory for at most 5 minutes, used to rate-limit room creation and WebSocket token issuance. Deleted when the challenge is consumed or expires.
  • Encrypted messages in transit. The server relays ciphertext (messages and images) between participants. It holds each encrypted message in memory only for the time needed to forward it to the other connected participant; nothing is queued for a participant who is not connected, and nothing is written to disk. The server cannot decrypt it (see Section 3). To detect replayed messages, the server keeps in memory a SHA-256 hash of each relayed ciphertext for the life of the room; the hash reveals nothing about the content.
  • Correspondence. If you write to the support or abuse addresses below, or if you receive the beta password from the Controller, the Controller processes your email address or messaging contact and the content of the exchange in order to reply, to distribute the password and, for abuse reports, to investigate.

The cookies and browser-storage entries described in this policy are strictly necessary to provide the service you request, or store a preference you have expressed, and therefore do not require your consent under Article 122 of the Italian Privacy Code and Article 5(3) of Directive 2002/58/EC.

What the Service does not process:

  • Message content in the clear. Messages are encrypted end-to-end in your browser. The Controller cannot read them.
  • Accounts or profile data. There is no registration. No name, email address, phone number, or other identifier is collected from users of the Service.
  • Analytics, trackers, advertising cookies, or fingerprinting. None are used.

What is stored in your browser and never sent to the Controller:

  • Identity keypair (IndexedDB, pinchat_identity_v1). An ECDSA P-256 keypair generated in your browser on first use and kept for up to 24 hours, matching the session lifetime; after that it is discarded and a fresh one is generated on next use. The private key is created as non-extractable inside the WebCrypto API, so it cannot be exported as raw bytes; note that a compromised page could still invoke it to produce signatures, which is why the Controller recommends verifying the Short Authentication String (SAS) with your contact out of band. The key is used only to sign your ephemeral Diffie-Hellman public keys, so that the SAS you verify with your contact remains stable across reconnects and tab refreshes within the same 24-hour window.
  • Bootstrap-key transit cache (sessionStorage). Immediately after the page loads, the room link's fragment (which carries the room bootstrap key) is moved from the address bar into tab-scoped sessionStorage and removed from the URL, so that the secret does not appear in browser history, screen shares, or window titles for the rest of the session. The same tab-scoped storage briefly holds the single-use WebSocket token between the moment a room is created and the moment the chat page connects.
  • Preferences (localStorage). Your light/dark theme choice and the fact that you dismissed the cookie notice. Both are plain flags with no identifier.

You can erase all of the above at any time from your browser's site-data settings. The identity entry also expires on its own after 24 hours, and the sessionStorage entries disappear when the tab is closed.

3. How Encryption Works, in Brief

The room link's fragment carries a 256-bit bootstrap key generated in the creator's browser. Browsers never send URL fragments to servers, so the Controller never receives it. Participants use the bootstrap key to encrypt and authenticate an initial Diffie-Hellman exchange, sign their ephemeral public keys with their local identity key, and derive per-message keys with a Double Ratchet, so that session keys rotate continuously and a compromised key does not expose past messages. Once the ratchet is running, the bootstrap key is discarded from memory. The SAS shown in the interface lets you confirm with your contact, over another channel, that no third party sits in between.

This design means the server only ever handles ciphertext. It also means the security of the scheme depends on the JavaScript delivered to your browser by pinchat.io: a compromised or malicious server could serve modified code. The source is published (see the Terms of Service) so that this can be audited, and an optional browser extension, published in the same repository, checks the files served by pinchat.io against a signed manifest before they run. Without the extension, the Controller cannot technically prove to you that the code served at any given moment matches a published revision.

6. Retention

  • Rooms and in-memory metadata: until the time-to-live you configure (5 minutes to 24 hours) elapses, counted from the room's creation. A room that all participants have left is kept, empty, until that moment and then removed. Never written to disk.
  • IP address: in the clear, for the duration of the request or connection only; as a keyed hash, for the rate-limit window, at most 15 minutes after your last request. No persistent logs.
  • Proof-of-work cookie and challenge cache: 5 minutes.
  • CSRF cookie: 15 minutes, or until access is granted.
  • Session cookie: 24 hours by default (sliding window), or until logout.
  • Ciphertext replay hashes: for the life of the room.
  • Local identity keypair (your browser): 24 hours from creation, or until you clear site data.
  • Beta password correspondence: for the duration of the beta phase, then deleted.
  • Support correspondence: up to 12 months from the last exchange, then deleted.
  • Abuse-report correspondence: up to (abuse report retention not configured) from resolution of the report, then deleted, unless a longer period is required by a pending legal proceeding.

8. Automated Decision-Making

The Service does not carry out profiling or automated decision-making producing legal or similarly significant effects within the meaning of Article 22 GDPR. Rate limiting and proof-of-work challenges are applied uniformly and do not evaluate personal characteristics.

9. Security

Technical measures in place include: HTTPS/TLS; Argon2id hashing of the beta access password; HMAC-signed CSRF tokens with constant-time verification; single-use, short-lived (30 seconds) JWT tokens for WebSocket upgrade; proof-of-work and rate limiting against automated abuse; end-to-end encryption of message content as described in Section 3; and a container running without root privileges, with all Linux capabilities dropped and a read-only root filesystem.

The Service has not undergone a formal third-party security audit and may contain vulnerabilities. Do not rely on it for anything you cannot afford to have exposed.
