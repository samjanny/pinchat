4. End-to-End Encryption and Content Responsibility

Messages and images exchanged through the Service are encrypted end-to-end in your browser. In normal operation the server only relays ciphertext and does not hold the keys needed to decrypt it, so the Operator does not have access to the content of your conversations and cannot moderate it proactively.

That property has limits, and it is not a guarantee:

  • The code your browser runs. The encryption is performed by JavaScript that the Service itself delivers. A server that had been compromised, or that was operated maliciously, could deliver altered code that reads messages before they are encrypted. Publishing the source code does not by itself prevent this.
  • The integrity extension. An optional browser extension, published with the source code, lets a browser run only the scripts of a signed release and reports files that differ from it. It narrows the risk above; it does not remove it. In Firefox it also checks each page before it is shown. Chromium-based browsers do not allow an extension to do that, and there the page itself is not attested. The extension depends on the Operator's signing key and on the channel it was installed from, it has not been independently audited, and none of it applies when it is not installed.
  • Who you are talking to. Encryption does not tell you whose keys you have agreed on. In a one-to-one room that is what the security code (Short Authentication String) is for: both participants compare it over another channel. If you skip that comparison the conversation is still encrypted, but someone able to interfere with the connection, which includes whoever controls the server, could place themselves between you and your contact without being noticed. Group rooms have no security code: anyone holding the link can join, and members are told apart only by the fingerprint of their key, which you can compare over another channel.
  • Your device and the people in the room. Nothing here protects you against a compromised device or browser, a malicious browser extension, or a participant who chooses to disclose the conversation.

These are things that are technically possible, not statements that any of them has happened. The Service has not been independently audited (Section 1).

The Operator does not normally have access to the content of conversations (Section 4). The Operator can nevertheless end rooms and connections and apply technical blocks, for example on network addresses.

If you believe the Service is being used to transmit illegal content or to harass you, report it to (abuse contact not configured) with enough context for an investigation: the room identifier, or the room link with everything from the # onwards removed, the nature of the violation, and the relevant timestamps. Never include the part of a room link that follows the #. That fragment is the room's encryption key. It is not sent to the Service when you open the link, and email is not encrypted, so pasting it into a report would disclose the key to the Operator and to every mail server in between. The application keeps no access log, and the reverse proxy in front of it is configured not to write access or error logs; neither keeps a log of who communicated with whom, in which room or when. Apart from start-up notices about its own configuration, the only log output the application produces is diagnostic error messages, written so as not to identify users, rooms or connections. What the hosting provider may record at network level is outside the Operator's control and is described in the Privacy Policy. Enforcement actions are therefore based on the information available at the time of the report; the Operator may be unable to act on reports concerning rooms that have already expired. A report does not guarantee that a violation can be established: the Operator usually has no content to examine, and the Service keeps no record showing who used a room.

The Operator may terminate any room or connection, and block any network address, that is reasonably suspected of violating these Terms. How that is done depends on the tools available at the time: at present the Service has no way of closing one room selectively, so ending a room may mean restarting the Service, which ends every room in progress.
